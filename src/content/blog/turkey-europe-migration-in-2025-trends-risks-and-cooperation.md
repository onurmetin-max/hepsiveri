<em>Irregular detections at the EU’s borders are lower year-on-year, but Turkey remains central; EU+ asylum applications are also down in 2025.</em>
<h2>Key figures at a glance (Jan–Oct 2025)</h2>
<strong>H1 2025 detections:</strong> <strong>≈75,900 (-20%)</strong> at the EU’s external borders <strong>(Frontex, 10 Jul 2025)</strong>. (<a title=" EU external borders: Irregular crossings drop by 20% in first half of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-drop-by-20-in-first-half-of-2025-CUpZ5o">Frontex</a>)

<strong>Eastern Mediterranean H1:</strong> <strong>≈19,600 detections</strong> <strong>(Frontex H1 2025)</strong>. (<a title=" EU external borders: Irregular crossings drop by 20% in first half of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-drop-by-20-in-first-half-of-2025-CUpZ5o">Frontex</a>)

<strong>Jan–Sep 2025:</strong> <strong>133,400 (-22%)</strong> irregular entries <strong>(latest Frontex update)</strong>. (<a title="irregular crossings fall 22% in the first 9 months of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-fall-22-in-the-first-9-months-of-2025-bUQtLl">Frontex</a>)

<strong>Italy sea arrivals:</strong> <strong>30,060 in H1 2025</strong> <strong>(UNHCR Italy dashboard)</strong>. (<a title="ITALY Sea arrivals dashboard" href="https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/download/118011">UNHCR Veri Portalı</a>)

<strong>Greece land arrivals 2025:</strong> <strong>4,293</strong> (updated <strong>5 Oct 2025</strong>, UNHCR ODP; sea/land on same page). (<a title=" Situation Europe Sea Arrivals " href="https://data.unhcr.org/en/situations/europe-sea-arrivals/location/24489">UNHCR Veri Portalı</a>)

The decline reflects both <strong>fewer successful crossings</strong> on some routes and <strong>policy effects</strong> from accelerated procedures—yet protection needs persist and claims are <strong>redistributing</strong> across destinations.
<h2>Inside Turkey: hosting, services, and pressure points</h2>
<div class="flourish-wrapper">
<div class="flourish-embed flourish-chart" data-src="visualisation/25581900"><noscript><img src="https://public.flourish.studio/visualisation/25581900/thumbnail" width="100%" alt="chart visualization" /></noscript></div>
</div>
Turkey remains the <strong>largest refugee-hosting country</strong>; by <strong>end-2024, ~3.1 million</strong> people were hosted <strong>(UNHCR country page; 2024 Annual Results Report)</strong>. (<a title="Republic of Türkiye | UNHCR" href="https://www.unhcr.org/us/where-we-work/countries/republic-tuerkiye">UNHCR</a>)

<strong>Syrians under temporary protection mid-2025:</strong> <strong>~2.61 million</strong> (PMM figures as cited in policy/academic analyses for <strong>15 Aug 2025</strong>). (<a title="Suriye'nin Yeniden İnşası Bağlamında Geri Dönüş ..." href="https://platform.ilke.org.tr/analiz/suriye-nin-yeniden-insasi-baglaminda-geri-donus-dinamikleri">Platform</a>)

The charts underline the <strong>scale</strong> (sustained pressure on municipal health/education/housing) and <strong>composition</strong> (Syrian predominance), requiring <strong>long-horizon local planning</strong>.
<h2>Routes and nationalities: how the map is shifting</h2>
Frontex notes a growing share of <strong>Afghan, Egyptian, and Bangladeshi</strong> nationals on the Eastern Mediterranean route and highlights the <strong>Libya–Crete corridor</strong> emerging in H1 2025. (<a title=" EU external borders: Irregular crossings drop by 20% in first half of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-drop-by-20-in-first-half-of-2025-CUpZ5o">Frontex</a>)
<h2>Policy in motion: EU–Turkey cooperation in 2025</h2>
Operational coordination and <strong>financing</strong> continued (FRIT and successor envelopes; municipal service support). (<a title="EU Support to Refugees in Türkiye" href="https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/enlargement-policy/turkiye/eu-support-refugees-turkiye_en">Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood</a>)
The aim remains to balance <strong>humanitarian protection</strong> with <strong>border management</strong>, expand <strong>legal pathways</strong>, and counter <strong>smuggling networks</strong>.
<h2>Economy and society: contribution vs. capacity</h2>
Refugees and migrants help fill labor gaps—especially in <strong>agriculture, construction, services</strong>—while <strong>housing/infrastructure</strong> capacity strains persist in high-pressure localities, keeping <strong>social cohesion</strong> on the agenda.
<h2>Route risks: fewer crossings, not fewer dangers</h2>
Enforcement/weather windows can shift journeys to <strong>longer, riskier sea legs</strong> (e.g., <strong>Eastern Libya–Crete</strong>), keeping <strong>SAR and safe disembarkation</strong> capacity in focus <strong>(Frontex H1 note; Missing Migrants reference)</strong>. (<a title=" EU external borders: Irregular crossings drop by 20% in first half of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-drop-by-20-in-first-half-of-2025-CUpZ5o">Frontex</a>)
<h2>How to read the numbers (method)</h2>
<strong>"Detections" ≠ "arrivals" ≠ "apprehensions."</strong> Frontex counts <strong>detections</strong> at EU external borders (same person may be detected multiple times); UNHCR reports <strong>arrivals</strong> by country; Turkish authorities report <strong>rescues/interceptions in national waters</strong> and <strong>inland apprehensions</strong>. (<a title=" EU external borders: Irregular crossings drop by 20% in first half of 2025" href="https://www.frontex.europa.eu/media-centre/news/news-release/eu-external-borders-irregular-crossings-drop-by-20-in-first-half-of-2025-CUpZ5o">Frontex</a>)
